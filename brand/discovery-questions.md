# Discovery Conversations — field guide

Three conversations, 15+ minutes each. Due before 20 Sep 2026.
Purpose: get the founder's positioning written in the owner's own words,
and test whether the assumed pain is the real one.

## Rules — read before every conversation

1. **Do not pitch.** The moment you describe the service, they start
   being polite and the data dies. If they ask what you do: "I'm working
   out whether there's a problem worth solving — can I ask you a few
   more things first?"
2. **Ask about the last time, not about generally.** "Tell me about the
   last time" produces memory. "Do you usually" produces theory.
3. **Shut up after the question.** Count to five in your head. The
   second thing they say is the true one.
4. **Write their words, not your summary.** "It does my head in" is
   copy. "Owner expressed frustration" is not.
5. **Never say "missed call", "automation", or "AI" first.** If those
   words appear, they must come out of their mouth, not yours.

## Opening (30 seconds)

"I'm looking at starting something for local businesses around here and
I'm trying to find out if I'd be solving a real problem or one I made
up. You're not a sales target — I'd just like fifteen minutes of how
your week actually runs. Can I ask you some questions?"

## Part 1 — How work arrives (3 min)

1. How do people get in touch with you? Walk me through all of them.
2. Which one brings you the most actual work?
3. Roughly how many enquiries come in a week?
4. What time of day do they come in?

*Listening for: whether phone genuinely dominates, and whether the
channels they name match the assumption in NORTH_STAR.*

## Part 2 — The mandatory question (5 min — the core of the whole thing)

5. **"When someone reaches out and you can't get to them right then —
   what happens?"**

Then say nothing. Let them fill it.

Follow with, one at a time, only as needed:

6. Tell me about the last time that happened. What were you doing?
7. What did you do about it afterwards?
8. Did that one come back, or did you lose it?
9. How did you find out you'd lost it?

*Write down every phrase verbatim. This is where the positioning
sentence comes from.*

## Part 3 — Mechanism or result? (3 min)

This section decides the name and the offer. Do not skip it.

10. Of the enquiries that don't turn into work — where do they usually
    fall over? At first contact, or later on?
11. What about the ones you do answer — what happens between the first
    call and the job actually being booked?
12. If you could hand one part of that to somebody else tomorrow, which
    part would it be?

*Listening for: if the loss is at first contact, a mechanism name
(Ringback, Callback) still works. If the loss is at quoting,
scheduling or follow-up, only a result name survives — and the offer
described in NORTH_STAR needs widening beyond missed calls.*

## Part 4 — Cost of the problem (3 min)

13. Roughly what's a job worth to you, on average?
14. How many do you reckon you've lost that way in the last month?
15. Has it ever cost you a big one?

*Do not correct their maths and do not flinch at a low number. A low
number is data — it means this niche is not the one.*

## Part 5 — Current workaround (2 min)

16. Have you tried anything to fix it?
17. What happened with that?
18. Is anyone else helping you with the phones or the bookings now?

*Listening for: what they already pay for, and what they have already
rejected. Both go into the anti-ICP.*

## Part 6 — The price probe (2 min)

Ask this LATE, and only after they have named a cost in Part 4.

19. "If something got you back two of those a month — what would that be
    worth to you?"

Let them name a number first. Never name A$350 before they answer.

20. If they ask what it would cost: "I'm not selling today. I'm working
    out if it's worth building. What would feel fair to you?"

*This is the Skeptic's objection from the council session being tested
in the field: does "missed calls" read as a commodity that anchors the
price below A$350, or as a real loss worth paying to stop?*

## Part 7 — Naming test (1 min)

21. "If you told a mate about a service that handles this for you —
    what would you call it?"

22. Say the shortlisted name out loud: "What do you think of if I say
    the word 'Nabbed'?" Watch the face before the words.

*The second question exists to test whether "nabbed" reads as
"caught by police" to an actual Australian tradie.*

## Closing

23. Who else around here has this problem worse than you?
24. Would you be open to me coming back once I've built something? No
    obligation — I'd want your honest reaction, not your money.

*Question 23 is how conversations 4 through 10 get booked. Do not skip
it. Question 24 is the pilot ask, softened.*

## After all three — before writing positioning

Count how many of the three named the same failure point in Part 3.

- **3 of 3 the same** → write positioning on it, with confidence.
- **2 of 3** → write it, and name the exception in the anti-ICP.
- **all different** → the niche list is too wide. Cut to the one with
  the highest average job value and re-run. Do not write positioning
  from three different problems.
