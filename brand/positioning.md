# Positioning — [NAME]

Written 30 Aug 2026, from three recorded discovery conversations
(brand/interviews/2026-08-30-round-1.md) and brand/market-pricing.md.

## 1. Positioning

> **"It answers the phone when I can't, and gets the quote back out
> before they ring the next bloke."**

That is the line. It is not written by us — it is assembled from what
Wayne and Marco actually said, and it covers both places the money
falls over: picking up, and quoting. A prospect who cannot repeat it
back has not understood us.

**The separator, in six words:** we recover the enquiries you already
paid to get. Marketing agencies sell you new traffic. A generic AI
receptionist takes a message. We do neither.

**Longer form, for the website and the one-pager — not for the pub:**
[NAME] installs and runs the system that answers, qualifies and quotes
every enquiry a Northern Rivers small business misses.

## 2. ICP

**Sector.** Tradies, hospitality and catering, multi-practitioner
wellness clinics, real estate, tourism operators. Northern Rivers NSW —
Ballina, Byron, Bangalow, Lennox Head, Lismore.

**The axis that decides the track — not the sector.** Does the business
already pay a human for the answering, admin or quoting function?

- **Paid anchor exists** (Marco: part-time person at $30/hr, ~$1,200/mo)
  → A$350/mo track. Their reference price is $1,000–$2,500 for a full
  Australian answering service. A$350 argues itself. This is where the
  quarter's paying client comes from.
- **No paid anchor** (Wayne: pays $0) → free pilot, capped at 2, in
  exchange for a documented case study with real numbers. Market rate
  for basic AI reception is $99–$149, so A$350 does not close here.
  Wayne's "fifty, hundred bucks a month" was market-accurate, not a
  lowball.

**Buying trigger.** A named, dated loss they can still feel. Marco's
40-person birthday, $1,800, "quoted late, they went with the place next
door." Wayne's $1,200 hot water system. Seasonal peaks sharpen it:
storm season Nov–Feb for tradies, spring wedding season for catering.

**Qualifying question the founder asks.** "Right now, who handles it
when an enquiry comes in and you're on the tools — do you pay anyone
for that, or does it just wait for you?" Pays someone = Track B.
Waits for them = pilot.

**Second qualifier, for scope.** "Where does it actually fall over —
picking up, or getting the quote back out?" Wayne: first contact.
Marco: "Me actually sitting down to write the quote. That's the
bottleneck, not the phone." Both are in scope. Sell the one they named.

## 3. Anti-ICP

**Solo wellness practitioner.** Sarah, remedial massage, Byron. Pays $0,
loses 2–3 bookings a month at $120–160, and rates the pain herself:
"Honestly it's fine, it happens. I don't stress about it too much."
No anchor, weak pain, and an identity objection we cannot argue away:
"I don't want a robot talking to my clients about their back pain."
Selling here costs three conversations to land a $50 maybe. Skip.
The wellness niche stays — the target inside it moves to
multi-practitioner clinics with a paid reception desk.

**Businesses wanting more leads.** Anyone whose stated problem is
traffic, SEO, ads or social. That is the six local marketing agencies'
job, not ours. We work on enquiries that already arrived.

**Anyone wanting a whole platform.** Wayne on GoHighLevel: "Looked
complicated as hell... I just want the phone thing sorted, not a whole
system." Marco on restaurant CRMs: "way too much for what I need."
If they want a system, we are the wrong shop.

## 4. The test sentence

> "It answers the phone when I can't, and gets the quote back out
> before they ring the next bloke."

Built from Wayne — "Answering the bloody phone. Or at least not losing
the ones I miss" and "If I don't get back to 'em quick they've rung the
next bloke on Google" — and Marco: "I just need the quoting bit sorted."
If a prospect cannot repeat this back in their own words, we have not
explained it.
