Discovery Questions

Field script · 15–20 minutes · Northern Rivers

Before you start

  1. Don't pitch. If you describe it, they start being polite and the data dies.
  2. Ask about the last time, never about "generally".
  3. After a question, shut up. Count to five. The second thing they say is the true one.
  4. Write their words, not your summary. Keep the swearing.
  5. Never say "missed call", "automation" or "AI" first. It has to come from them.

Opening

Say this Thanks for giving me a few minutes. I'm looking at starting something for local businesses around here, and before I build anything I want to find out whether I'd be solving a real problem or one I've invented in my own head. So this isn't a sales thing — I'm not going to try to sell you anything today. I just want fifteen minutes on how your week actually runs. Is that alright?
If they ask what you're building Honestly, that's what I'm trying to work out. If I tell you now you'll start being polite about it and I'll learn nothing. Can I ask you a few things first, and I'll tell you properly at the end?

1 · How work arrives

1How do people get in touch with you? Walk me through all of them.
2Which one actually brings you the most work?
3Roughly how many enquiries come in over a week?
4What time of day do they tend to come in?
5Is there a time of year where it goes mental?

2 · The core question

6. When someone reaches out and you can't get to them right then — what happens?

Then stop talking. Count to five. Let them fill the silence.

7Tell me about the last time that happened. What were you actually doing at the time?
8What did you do about it afterwards?
9Did that one come back to you, or did you lose it?
10How did you find out you'd lost it?
11How did that sit with you?
12Does that happen much?

3 · Where it falls over

13Think about the enquiries that don't turn into work. Where do they usually fall over — right at the start, or somewhere later on?
14And the ones you do get to — what has to happen between that first call and the job actually being in the diary?
15How long does that usually take?
16Where does that get stuck?
17If you could hand one part of all that to somebody else tomorrow and never think about it again — which part would you pick?

This section decides the name and the offer. Loss at first contact → a mechanism name still works. Loss at quoting or follow-up → only a result name survives.

4 · What it costs

18Roughly, what's an average job worth to you?
19How many do you reckon you've lost that way in the last month or so?
20Has it ever cost you a big one? What happened?
21Do you ever find out about the ones you lost, or do they just disappear?

Don't correct their maths and don't flinch at a low number. A low number is data — it means this niche isn't the one.

5 · What they've already tried

22Have you tried doing anything about it?
23How did that go?
24Is anyone helping you with the phones or the bookings at the moment?
25What do you pay for that?
26Is there anything you've looked at and thought, nah, not for me?
27What put you off?

6 · Worth

Only after they've given you a number in Part 4. Don't say a price. Let them go first.

28. Say something got you back two of those a month. What would that be worth to you?
If they ask what you'd charge I'm genuinely not selling today — I don't even have it built yet. I'm trying to work out whether it's worth building. What would feel fair to you?
If they push again I'd rather hear your number than give you mine, or I'll just anchor you to whatever I say.

7 · The name

29If you were telling a mate about a service that handled this for you — what would you call it?
30What comes to mind if I say the word “Nabbed”?
31And “Nothing Missed”?

Watch the face before the words. A polite person corrects their reaction in half a second — the first half-expression is the real answer.

Closing

32That's been really useful, thank you. Who else around here do you reckon has this problem worse than you?
33Would you mind if I mentioned you'd sent me?
34Once I've actually built something, would you be open to me coming back and showing you? No obligation and no cost — I'd want your honest reaction more than your money.
If they now ask what it is — then stop selling I set up a system that answers people when you can't, and gets them booked in. That's it. You stay in charge of the work — it just means nobody sits there waiting on you and gives up.

In the car, before you drive off

Five minutes. Write it down while it's fresh.

  1. Their exact words, in quotes. Not your summary.
  2. Where the loss happens: first contact / quoting / scheduling / follow-up.
  3. Average job value, number lost per month, worst single loss, what they said it'd be worth.
  4. Their face when you said “Nabbed”.
  5. Names they gave you for question 32.

The words matter more than your summary. If they swore, keep the swearing — that's the line that goes on the website.
