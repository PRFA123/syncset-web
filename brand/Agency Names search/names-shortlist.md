# Name Shortlist — decision due 20 Sep 2026

The name must say "you stop losing the calls you miss" to a tradie on a noisy job site, without sounding like a Sydney software company.

**Ringback**
- Why: Names the outcome in one plain word every tradie already uses.
- Risk: Generic telco flavour; may collide with existing call-centre products.
- To verify: ringback.com.au / @ringback — to verify

**Never Miss**
- Why: The customer's own complaint, said back to them; easy over the phone.
- Risk: Two words, common phrase — weak trademark, hard to own in search.
- To verify: nevermiss.com.au / @nevermiss — to verify

**Second Ring**
- Why: Suggests the follow-up that happens when nobody picked up the first time.
- Risk: Could read as an engagement-ring or jewellery business.
- To verify: secondring.com.au / @secondring — to verify

**Callback Creek**
- Why: Northern Rivers flavour plus the literal service; local, memorable, spellable.
- Risk: Regional tone may cap it if he ever sells outside the Northern Rivers.
- To verify: callbackcreek.com.au / @callbackcreek — to verify

**Pickup**
- Why: One syllable, spoken daily, covers both answering the phone and winning the job.
- Risk: Heavily overloaded word (utes, deliveries, dating) — SEO and handle scarcity.
- To verify: pickup.com.au / @pickupnr — to verify

Recommendation: Callback Creek — because it is the only one that is both plainly descriptive and ownable, and local roots are an asset while the founder is selling face-to-face in the Northern Rivers.

## Review of an external naming analysis — "Nabbed", 2026-08-30

The founder brought a general naming analysis of "Nabbed" from another
source. Recorded here with what it got right and where it does not apply
to this studio.

**What it got right.** It independently identified the police-slang
reading ("he got nabbed by the cops"). That makes two sources reaching
the same risk from different directions — it stopped being a hunch.
It also produced a usable hook: "nabbed back your time".

**Where it misdiagnoses.** It frames the slang as a risk because the
name would sound "too informal for a traditional corporate B2B
audience". This studio's audience is not corporate B2B — it is tradies,
cafes, therapists and agents in the Northern Rivers. For them informal
is an asset. The real risk is the specific criminal reading, not
informality. Right fact, wrong reason.

**Where it inverts a strength into a weakness.** It lists "does not
signal AI" as a risk and proposes the tagline "Nabbed | AI & Automation
Studio". That contradicts the council decision of the same date: keep AI
out of the shopfront because it pre-loads the "is a robot going to talk
to my customers?" objection in every sales conversation. Not signalling
AI is the feature, not the defect.

**Where it reopens a settled question.** Its verdict says an abstract
name like Vektor carries more weight for "traditional corporate,
finance or industrial operations". That is the exact question already
taken to the council, with recorded dissent, and decided. The analysis
had no knowledge of the ICP, the zero client count, or the 20 Sep
deadline.

**One factual correction.** "Nabbed" is one syllable, not two.

## Brand voice note — if a capture-verb name is ever used

"Nabbed your leads" works when said to the business owner. The same verb
aimed at their customer has a predatory edge — a massage client should
never feel nabbed. Any capture verb in this brand must point at the
opportunity, never at the person.

## FIELD VERDICT — three conversations, 30 Aug 2026

Both the analysis above and the earlier council session were superseded
by testing the names out loud on three real business owners. Transcripts
in `brand/interviews/2026-08-30-round-1.md`.

**"Nabbed" — rejected 3 out of 3.**
- Wayne (plumber): "like nabbing crooks? Dunno if that fits plumbing
  mate, haha." Confused, then amused. Not offended — just did not land.
- Sarah (massage): "doesn't really feel like me, a bit aggressive for
  wellness."
- Marco (cafe/catering): "sounds a bit like a parking fine app, not
  gonna lie."

Three independent people, three different trades, three variations of
the same objection — and the criminal reading surfaced unprompted twice.
This is the risk flagged in the single-word sweep, confirmed in the
field. Nabbed is dead.

**"Nothing Missed" — 2 of 3 positive, with visible reaction.**
- Wayne: "yeah, that one actually makes sense for what you're
  describing."
- Marco: face changed. "That's closer, especially with the quote thing
  too." Note that it stretched to cover a pain that is NOT missed calls
  — which is exactly the durability the council amendment was after.
- Sarah: neutral. "Fine I guess, doesn't excite me either way."

**Unprompted, both strong respondents reached for the same shape.**
Wayne would call it "Never Miss a Call or somethin' obvious like that".
Marco said "something about not losing the booking". Neither invented an
abstract name. Both went to plain description of the result.
