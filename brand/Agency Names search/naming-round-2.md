# Naming round 2 — revised brief

Supersedes the criteria in `naming-brief.md`, which were derived from
simulated interviews and a single-region, trades-first ICP.

## What changed

The earlier brief assumed a local Northern Rivers studio selling to
tradies face to face. That premise came from simulated personas and no
longer holds. The revised brief is:

- A multi-niche automation agency, not a single-vertical studio
- International in ambition, not Australia-only
- Professional and credible to a business buyer
- Technology association is acceptable and may be an asset
- Not a quirky or invented-sounding word

## Criteria that survive

1. **One word strongly preferred**, or a compound that reads as one.
2. **Sayable and spellable** on a first hearing.
3. **Works as a handle** without hyphens or underscores.
4. **Not locked to a region** — this one strengthens under the new brief.
5. **Portable across TLDs** — founder's residency is still unresolved,
   so a `.com` or `.ai` matters more than a `.com.au`.

## Criteria that are dropped

- "Must be pronounceable by a tradie on a noisy job site" — that buyer
  was invented.
- "No AI, tech, or abstract terms" — the objection this protected
  against came from a simulated persona.
- "Must name the result, not the mechanism" — this came from a
  simulated caterer whose pain was quoting.

## Criterion added

**Credibility at scale.** The name has to survive being on a proposal
sent to a mid-market company, not only a business card handed to a
sole trader.

## Reality check before verifying

Every name above is a single English or Latin dictionary word. Round 1
established, by RDAP, that this class is exhausted — sixteen single
words tested in `.com.au` were all registered, and eight of eight
descriptive words in `.com` were registered.

So the bare `.com` for Relay, Aegis, Cadence, Axiom and Vertex is
almost certainly taken and, where free, priced as a premium asset.
That is not a reason to drop the shortlist. It means the realistic
forms are:

1. **`.ai` on the bare word** — a much younger namespace, and now
   acceptable under the revised brief. `relay.ai`, `aegis.ai`.
2. **Compound with a work noun** — `relayworks`, `aegisgroup`,
   `cadencelabs`, `fulcrumhq`.
3. **Compound with a second concept** — `relaypoint`, `sentrydesk`,
   `throughlineops`.

Option 1 is the strongest under this brief: shortest, most credible at
scale, and portable regardless of residency.

## Round 2b — concrete objects, single word

**The insight:** the AI rush took the abstract Latin and Greek nouns —
Vertex, Axiom, Aegis, Nexus, Apex, Meridian. It did not take concrete
physical objects. That is where single-word availability still exists,
and the right metaphor for this business lives there anyway: the thing
that stops something being dropped.

| Name | Why | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| **Baton** | The relay baton — literally the object that must not be dropped in a handoff. "Relay" being taken makes this the undisputed sibling. | Slight sporting flavour; French homograph. |
| **Dovetail** | The joint that holds without nails, and the verb for two things fitting perfectly. Says integration without saying it. | Eight letters, two syllables written as one word. |
| **Rivet** | Holds structure under load. Hard consonants, memorable. "Riveting" is a positive second sense. | Industrial tone may read heavy for services. |
| **Latch** | Catches and holds. One syllable, physical, direct. | Short words are the most contested. |
| **Cinch** | To secure tightly, and "it's a cinch" — easy. Rare double meaning: what it does and how it feels. | Informal register; may read light for enterprise. |
| **Keel** | What keeps the vessel stable in bad weather. Short, solid, no tech odour. | Nautical association is generic. |

**Excluded from this family, already known taken by large companies:**
Loom (video), Tether (crypto), Ledger (crypto wallet), Thread (widely
used in software), Anchor, Compass, Pillar (all generic and crowded).

**Batch 5 — verify when network returns**
baton · dovetail · rivet · latch · cinch · keel
Across `.com`, `.ai`, and `.com.au`. Then trade marks at IP Australia
and USPTO for survivors.
