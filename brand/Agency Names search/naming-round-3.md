# Naming round 3 — three forms

Rounds 1 and 2 searched the most contested namespace there is: single
short words in `.com` and `.ai`. Over fifty were checked and all were
taken. What survived — Zent, Zenit, AptivAI — survived because nobody
wanted it.

The correction: **availability is the last filter, not the driver.**
These three forms open real space without giving up professionalism.

---

## Form 1 — natural two-word phrases

Not tech composites like NexusCore. Real expressions people already
say. Far less contested, and this is how many strong agency brands are
named.

| Name | Why | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| **Front Foot** | "On the front foot" — ahead of it, proactive. That is exactly the shift the service delivers: you stop being behind every enquiry. British and Australian business idiom, understood instantly by a lawyer and a tradie. | Idiom is weaker in the US market. Sporting origin (cricket). |
| **Safe Hands** | "In safe hands." Trust in two words. Strongest of the set for professional services, still plain enough for trades. | Warm rather than technical; may read as care or medical. |
| **Standing Order** | A real banking and business term: a recurring instruction that executes without being re-asked. That is literally what automation is. Sophisticated, and it flatters the buyer's intelligence. | Banking association is strong. Two words, thirteen letters. |
| **Second Nature** | Work that used to take attention now happens without it. Describes the outcome of good automation precisely. | Common phrase; likely contested. |
| **Even Keel** | "On an even keel" — steady, nothing capsizes. Uses the keel idea idiomatically, which is more ownable than the bare word. | Nautical; calm rather than dynamic. |
| **Open Line** | The line is never closed. Direct, simple, telecoms-adjacent without being telecom. | "Line" narrows toward phone specifically. |

**Strongest three to verify: Front Foot, Safe Hands, Standing Order.**

## Form 2 — distinctive word plus category

The pattern behind names like "Merrick Labs". The category word does
the professional work; the distinctive word does the memory work. Also
much easier to clear on domains, because the pair is unique even when
the root is not.

| Name | Why | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| **Anvil Works** | An anvil is where things are made and where force is absorbed. Concrete, industrial, unpretentious, and not colonised by SaaS. | Heavy industrial tone for a services buyer. |
| **Kindred Systems** | Kindred — of the same kind, working together. Speaks to human-plus-system rather than replacement. | "Kindred" is used in gambling and healthcare. |
| **Forge Collective** | A forge is where raw material becomes a tool. Strong verb-noun. | "Collective" implies more than one person. |
| **Lantern Studio** | Something that shows the way in the dark. Warm, professional, memorable. | Soft; used in education and finance. |
| **Quarry Works** | Where the raw material comes from. Unusual, ownable, concrete. | Extraction metaphor can read as taking rather than giving. |

**Note on category words.** "Labs", "Works" and "Studio" are safe for a
solo founder — they describe a place, not a headcount. Avoid
"Partners", "Collective" and "Group": they claim people you do not
have, and the claim collapses in the first serious conversation.

## Form 3 — strong name, longer domain

The self-imposed rule that the domain must be a short bare word is what
produced Zent. Dropping it recovers names that were rejected only on
domain grounds.

| Approach | Example | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Verb prefix | `gethandoff.com`, `getfrontfoot.com` | Standard SaaS convention; reads as intentional, not as a fallback. |
| Category suffix | `relayworks.com`, `handoffstudio.com` | Keeps the strong root, makes the pair ownable. |
| New TLD used properly | `handoff.studio`, `frontfoot.agency` | Works when the TLD completes the phrase rather than replacing `.com`. |
| Australian anchor | `frontfoot.com.au` | Only if residency resolves — auDA requires continuing Australian presence. |

**The trade honestly stated:** a longer domain costs a few characters
in an email address. A meaningless name costs an explanation in every
sales conversation, permanently. The second is far more expensive.

## Verification queue — round 3

Run RDAP first, then IP Australia and USPTO on survivors.

**Priority 1:** frontfoot · safehands · standingorder
**Priority 2:** secondnature · evenkeel · openline
**Priority 3:** anvilworks · kindredsystems · forgecollective ·

**Across:** `.com`, `.com.au`, `.ai`, plus the Form 3 variants for
anything that survives.

**Do not report availability from anything but RDAP.** Every prior
round in this project was polluted by assertions dressed as checks.

## Trade mark check — 30 Aug 2026, IP Australia

Both domain-verified survivors from this round are dead on trade mark
grounds. Recording this because it changes the process order below.

**"Front Foot" — DEAD.** Registered mark #2294043, "Front Foot",
owner FrontFoot Consolidated Pty Ltd, classes 9, 16, 35, 41, 42, 45.
Status: Registered/protected. Class 42 is computer and technology
services — the exact space this business would file in. "Front Foot
Studio" would likely read as a confusing variant of a live, protected
mark in the identical class.

**"Safe Hands" — DEAD.** 50 results, 22 registered. Two fatal ones:
- "SAFEHANDS" (word mark #2080147), classes 9 and 45, owner Safe Hands
  Holdings Pty Ltd. Class 9 is software/computer goods.
- "Safe Hands Family Trust" holds three registered marks in classes 9,
  35, 41, 42 — already operating in software/SaaS/business services
  under that name, even though the registered words differ.
Also crowded by Bridgestone ("You're in Safe Hands", tyres) and Safe
Hands Insurance Group.

## Process correction

Domain availability and trade mark availability are decoupled markets.
A domain can sit unregistered for years while the phrase is a live,
protected mark — nobody squats domains for marks they already own by
reputation. Checking domain first and trade mark second means falling
in love with names that were always going to die.

**New order, from here: trade mark first, domain second.** The IP
Australia search is free and faster than RDAP. Do not report a domain
as "available" as if that were good news until the mark has cleared.
