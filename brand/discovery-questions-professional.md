# Discovery — professional services variant

For law firms, accountants and BAS agents. Two conversations.

**The tradie script does not work here and must not be reused.** It asks
what happens when nobody answers. These offices have reception — the
phone gets answered. If a loss exists, it happens after that.

**What these two conversations decide:** whether professional services
is the same product sold to a new sector, or a different product. That
answer settles the ICP question, and the ICP question settles the name.

**Do not lead.** The whole point is to find out whether the pain we
built for even exists here. If it does not, that is the finding.

---

## Opening

"I'm building something for local businesses around enquiry handling,
and before I go further I want to check whether firms like yours have
the problem I think they do — or whether I've got it wrong.

I'm not selling anything today. Fifteen minutes on how new enquiries
actually move through your office. Would that be alright?"

---

## Part 1 — How new work arrives

1. "How does a new client enquiry usually reach you?"

2. "Who picks it up first?"

3. "How many new enquiries would you get in a week?"

4. "What proportion of those are actually a fit for you?"

5. "Is there a time of year it spikes?"

*For accountants, expect EOFY and BAS quarters. For law, expect it to
be event-driven rather than seasonal.*

---

## Part 2 — The core question, adapted

6. **"Walk me through what happens from the moment a new enquiry comes
   in to the moment it becomes an actual matter or engagement."**

Then stop. Let them narrate the whole chain. The gap is in the chain.

7. "Where in that does it usually slow down?"

8. "Has one ever fallen through the cracks?"

9. "Tell me about the last time. What happened?"

10. "How did you find out?"

11. "Do you think you'd know if one went quiet, or would it just not
    show up?"

*This is the equivalent of Marco's "I'll never know". Invisible loss is
the strongest buying trigger there is, because it cannot be argued with.*

---

## Part 3 — The anchor, which here is explicit

These offices already pay for the function. That is the whole reason
they might be a better ICP than a solo tradie. Get the number.

12. "Who covers the front desk?"

13. "Is that a full-time role, or shared with other work?"

14. "What hours does that cover?"

15. "What happens to an enquiry that lands outside those hours?"

16. "What about when they're on leave, or on another call?"

17. "Roughly what does that seat cost you all up?"

*Question 17 is the one that sets your price ceiling. Ask it plainly.
If they deflect, ask "ballpark — is it more like forty thousand a year
or more like eighty?"*

---

## Part 4 — Where the real friction is

Do not assume it is the phone. For these firms it is more likely to be
qualification, conflict checks, or the engagement paperwork.

18. "Of the enquiries that aren't a fit, how do you find that out?"

19. "How much time goes into working that out?"

20. "What has to happen before you can actually take someone on?"

21. "How long does that usually take, start to finish?"

22. "If you could hand one step of that to someone else tomorrow, which
    would it be?"

---

## Part 5 — What it's worth

23. "What's a typical new matter or engagement worth to you?"

24. "How many would you say slipped away last year — not lost on price,
    just never went anywhere?"

25. "Has one ever got away that you still think about?"

26. "If you got two more of those a year, what would that be worth?"

*Note the unit: for these firms it is per year, not per month. A law
firm losing two matters a year at $8,000 each is a bigger number than
Wayne's whole problem — but it feels smaller to them because it is rare.
Do not correct them. Write down how they frame it.*

---

## Part 6 — Trust, which is the real objection here

This is where professional services differ most. Their risk is not a
missed job — it is a client relationship and a regulator.

27. "If something automated handled a first enquiry for you, what would
    worry you about that?"

28. "Is there anything that absolutely could not be automated in your
    line of work?"

29. "Would you want the client to know they were talking to a system?"

*Listen hard here. If the answer is "our clients expect a person", that
is the same objection Sarah gave, and it kills the sector regardless of
the anchor.*

---

## Closing

30. "Who else around here would have a view on this?"

31. "Once I've built something, would you be open to me showing you?
    No cost, I'd want your honest reaction."

---

## Decision rule — apply after both conversations

This is what the two conversations are for. Be strict.

**Same product** — if both describe losing enquiries at first contact
or in the gap outside reception hours, and neither raises a blocking
trust objection. Then professional services joins the niche list, and
the name must work for a law firm as well as a plumber.

**Different product** — if both describe the friction at qualification,
conflict checking or engagement paperwork rather than at contact. Then
this is a second product, not a fifth niche. It does not belong in
Phase 1, and the name should be chosen for the sector you are actually
selling to now.

**Not an ICP** — if either says their clients expect a human and they
would not automate first contact. That is Sarah's objection with a
practising certificate. A paid anchor does not survive an identity
objection.

**If the two disagree with each other**, that is not a tie — it is
n=1 twice. Do a third before changing anything.

---

## Note on why this matters more than the name

The naming debate cannot be settled while this is open. A name that
suits a plumber and a name that suits a law firm are different names,
and picking one before deciding who you sell to is picking blind.

Two coffees settle it. That is cheaper than any amount of analysis.
