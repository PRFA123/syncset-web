# Discovery Script — read-aloud version

Every question written out in full, in the order you ask them.
No notes, no analysis — just the words. The reasoning behind each
section lives in `discovery-questions.md`.

Print this, or open it on your phone. Tick questions off as you go.

---

## Opening

"Thanks for giving me a few minutes.

I'm looking at starting something for local businesses around here, and
before I build anything I want to find out whether I'd be solving a real
problem or one I've invented in my own head.

So this isn't a sales thing — I'm not going to try to sell you anything
today. I just want fifteen minutes on how your week actually runs.

Is that alright?"

**If they ask what you're building:**

"Honestly, that's what I'm trying to work out. If I tell you now, you'll
start being polite about it and I'll learn nothing. Can I ask you a few
things first, and I'll tell you properly at the end?"

---

## Part 1 — How work arrives

1. "How do people get in touch with you? Walk me through all of them."

2. "Which one of those actually brings you the most work?"

3. "Roughly how many enquiries come in over a week?"

4. "What time of day do they tend to come in?"

5. "Is there a time of year where it goes mental?"

---

## Part 2 — The core question

Ask question 6. Then stop talking. Count to five.

6. **"When someone reaches out and you can't get to them right then —
   what happens?"**

Then, one at a time, only as the conversation needs them:

7. "Tell me about the last time that happened. What were you actually
   doing at the time?"

8. "What did you do about it afterwards?"

9. "Did that one come back to you, or did you lose it?"

10. "How did you find out you'd lost it?"

11. "How did that sit with you?"

12. "Does that happen much?"

---

## Part 3 — Where it falls over

13. "Think about the enquiries that don't turn into work. Where do they
    usually fall over — right at the start, or somewhere later on?"

14. "And the ones you do get to — what has to happen between that first
    call and the job actually being in the diary?"

15. "How long does that usually take?"

16. "Where does that get stuck?"

17. "If you could hand one part of all that to somebody else tomorrow
    and never think about it again — which part would you pick?"

---

## Part 4 — What it costs

18. "Roughly, what's an average job worth to you?"

19. "And how many do you reckon you've lost that way in the last month
    or so?"

20. "Has it ever cost you a big one? What happened?"

21. "Do you ever find out about the ones you lost, or do they just
    disappear?"

---

## Part 5 — What they've already tried

22. "Have you tried doing anything about it?"

23. "How did that go?"

24. "Is anyone helping you with the phones or the bookings at the
    moment?"

25. "What do you pay for that?"

26. "Is there anything you've looked at and thought, nah, not for me?"

27. "What put you off?"

---

## Part 6 — Worth

Only ask this after they have given you a number in Part 4.
Do not say a price. Let them go first.

28. "Say something got you back two of those a month. What would that be
    worth to you?"

**If they turn it around and ask what you'd charge:**

"I'm genuinely not selling today — I don't even have it built yet. I'm
trying to work out whether it's worth building. What would feel fair to
you?"

**If they push again:**

"I'd rather hear your number than give you mine, or I'll just anchor you
to whatever I say."

---

## Part 7 — The name

29. "If you were telling a mate about a service that handled this for
    you — what would you call it?"

30. "What comes to mind if I say the word 'Nabbed'?"

Watch their face before they answer. The face is the answer.

31. "And 'Nothing Missed'?"

---

## Closing

32. "That's been really useful — thank you.

    Who else around here do you reckon has this problem worse than you?"

33. "Would you mind if I mentioned you'd sent me?"

34. "Last thing. Once I've actually built something, would you be open
    to me coming back and showing you? No obligation and no cost — I'd
    want your honest reaction more than your money."

**If they now ask what it is, answer plainly and briefly:**

"I set up a system that answers people when you can't, and gets them
booked in. That's it. You stay in charge of the work — it just means
nobody sits there waiting on you and gives up."

Then stop. Do not keep selling.

---

## Straight after — before you drive off

Sit in the car and write it down while it is fresh. Five minutes.
Use the capture sheet in `discovery-questions.md`.

The words matter more than your summary. Write what they said, not what
you think they meant. If they swore, keep the swearing — that is the
line that goes on the website.
